# At Home Kenya — Discovery Call Script & Case Study Guidelines

## 1. CALL OBJECTIVES (know these before you dial)

In priority order:
1. Get their genuine buy-in that this is a real upgrade, not a sales trap — free only works if it doesn't feel like bait.
2. Understand their actual pain points and business goals (don't assume — the master prompt has hypotheses, this call validates them).
3. Get explicit permission to use them as a public case study, and agree what "public" means.
4. Get access: current site login/hosting info, brand assets, analytics (GA/Meta Pixel if any), and a contact who can approve copy/design decisions.
5. Set a timeline and a single point of contact on their side.

Do not try to close everything on this call. The goal is trust + information + access, not a signed contract on turn one.

---

## 2. PRE-CALL PREP (do this first)

- [ ] Pull their current site up in a second tab so you can reference it live.
- [ ] Note 2–3 specific things you genuinely like about what they've built (real ones — e.g. the WhatsApp-first CTA habit is smart, keep it).
- [ ] Note 2–3 specific gaps you'll bring up as *questions*, not verdicts (e.g. "I noticed pricing isn't shown anywhere — was that a deliberate choice?").
- [ ] Know their stats cold: 400+ families, 800+ visits, 100+ caregivers — you should sound like you've done homework, not like you're reading a pitch deck at them.
- [ ] Decide internally: what's the minimum viable "yes" you need from this call? (Usually: access + case study permission + a second call date.)

## 3. THE SCRIPT

Use this as a skeleton, not a word-for-word read. Bracketed sections are for you to fill with real names/details.

### Opening (30–60 sec)
> "Hi [Name], thanks for making time. I'll keep this short — we're a Nairobi-based digital agency, and we're building out a small portfolio of case studies with local businesses we genuinely respect. At Home Kenya came up because [specific reason — e.g. 'a friend used your service for her mother last year and had a great experience']. We'd like to redesign your website end-to-end, at no cost, in exchange for being able to document the process and results publicly. Before I get into any of that — can I ask you a few questions about how the business is actually running day to day?"

Why open this way: leads with *why them specifically* (not generic outreach), states the offer plainly and early (no bait-and-switch feel), and immediately hands the conversation back to them. People trust conversations more than pitches.

### Discovery questions (this is the real value of the call — spend most of your time here)

**On the business:**
- "Walk me through how a typical client finds you and books a caregiver today — website, WhatsApp, referral, phone?"
- "What percentage of your bookings would you say come through the website versus word-of-mouth or repeat clients?"
- "Do you get inquiries from family members abroad arranging care for someone here? How do those usually come in?"

**On current pain:**
- "What's the most common question or hesitation you hear from a new family before they book?"
- "Is there anything about the current site that actively frustrates you or your team?"
- "Do you track where bookings come from at all right now — any analytics, or is it mostly gut feel?"

**On goals:**
- "If this redesign works perfectly, what does that look like for you in 3 months? More bookings? More diaspora clients? Easier for your team to manage?"
- "Is there anything you'd explicitly *not* want to change — brand elements, tone, existing content?"

Listen for: whether their real bottleneck is trust/credibility, volume of leads, diaspora reach, or operational (e.g., WhatsApp overload, no booking system). This should shape what you build, even against your existing draft plan — the master prompt is a hypothesis, this call is where you correct it.

### Presenting the offer (after discovery, not before)
> "Based on what you're telling me, here's roughly what we're thinking — [give a 60-second, non-technical version: trust-first design, clearer service pages, WhatsApp kept front and center, a proper booking flow, and something built specifically for families arranging care from abroad]. This is free because we want a strong, honest case study — meaning we'll want to show before/after screenshots, talk about the strategy behind decisions, and ideally share some results once it's live. Nothing goes public without you seeing and approving it first."

### The case study ask (be specific — vague permission causes problems later)
> "For the case study specifically, here's what we'd like, and you can push back on any of it:
> - Screenshots of the current site and the new one, side by side
> - A short quote from you about the experience working with us — you'll approve the exact wording
> - If you're comfortable, some anonymized or aggregate performance numbers after launch — like booking inquiries or WhatsApp click-throughs, not private financials
> - Your logo and business name on our site and portfolio
> - We would not publish anything involving actual patient or family data — that stays completely out of it"

Explicitly ruling out patient data yourself, unprompted, is a strong trust signal — it shows you understand what shouldn't be touched before they have to ask.

### Access & logistics
> "To get started, we'd need: access to your current hosting/domain or at least confirmation of where it's hosted, any brand assets you have — logo files, photos you like, your caregiver photos if we can use them with their consent — and one point of contact on your side who can give quick yes/no answers on copy and design as we go, so we're not waiting a week between decisions."

### Close
> "Here's what I'd suggest as next steps: I'll send a short one-page agreement covering the free-work-for-case-study arrangement and what we're each committing to, plus a simple content/access checklist. Can we pencil in a follow-up call in [X days] once you've had a look?"

## 4. HANDLING LIKELY OBJECTIONS

**"What's the catch?"**
> "Fair question. The catch is genuinely just the case study — we're early, we need proof of work with a real business, and 'free for a good story, told honestly' is the trade. If it doesn't feel right at any point, we stop — no hard feelings."

**"We don't want our numbers public."**
> "Totally fine — we can keep it qualitative (what changed, why, what it looks like) without any specific figures, or use ranges/percentages instead of raw numbers if you're open to that."

**"How long will this take?"**
> Give a real estimate based on your own capacity — don't overpromise. Something like: "Realistically 3–5 weeks for a build like this, assuming we get quick turnarounds on approvals from your side."

**"We already have a developer/agency."**
> "No problem at all — this doesn't have to replace anything, we can build it as an option to compare against what you have, and you decide whether to switch."

## 5. AFTER THE CALL — CASE STUDY FRAMEWORK

### What to document, from day one (not after launch)
1. **Baseline snapshot** — current site screenshots (desktop + mobile), current analytics if they have any, current stated pain points (use their exact words from the call).
2. **Strategy rationale** — write down *why* you made each major decision while it's fresh (e.g. "we added a diaspora page because 30% of their inbound inquiries in the call turned out to be from family abroad, which wasn't reflected anywhere on the old site"). This is the part that makes a case study credible instead of just a portfolio piece.
3. **Process artifacts** — wireframes/design iterations, especially anything the client pushed back on and why (real case studies show iteration, not just a finished product).
4. **Launch snapshot** — new site screenshots, same views as baseline for direct comparison.
5. **Post-launch results** (agree a window, e.g. 30–60 days) — whatever you got permission for: WhatsApp click-throughs, booking form submissions, time-on-site, bounce rate, or a client quote if numbers aren't shareable.

### Permissions checklist (get this in writing, even informally over email/WhatsApp)
- [ ] Confirmed okay to use business name and logo publicly
- [ ] Confirmed okay to use before/after screenshots
- [ ] Confirmed what performance data (if any) can be shared, and in what form (exact numbers vs. ranges vs. none)
- [ ] Confirmed testimonial quote wording, approved by them
- [ ] Confirmed caregiver/staff photo usage rights if any real photos are used
- [ ] Explicit agreement that no patient/client personal data appears anywhere

### Case study structure (for when you write it up)
1. Client + one-line context
2. The problem (in their words, from the call)
3. What you built and why (tie back to discovery, not generic "we designed a beautiful site")
4. Before/after visuals
5. Results (numbers if you have them, qualitative outcome if you don't)
6. Client quote
7. CTA — for people reading it who want the same thing

In the meantime, if you can share [logo files / current hosting info / any photos you like], that'll help us move fast. Looking forward to building something you're proud to have your name on."
